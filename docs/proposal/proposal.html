A Thousand and One Cities

ألف مدينة ومدينة
Project document · revised November 2026 · the website sections now describe the platform page by page

1. Project Description

“A Thousand and One Cities” (ألف مدينة ومدينة) is a tourism and cultural project inspired by One Thousand and One Nights, based on the idea that every city has stories and experiences worth discovering.

The project presents cities differently from traditional tourist guides: through short, visual content that introduces a city through its food, music, heritage, places, customs and local experiences, together with suggested trips and itineraries for different budgets.

The core idea is:

Every city deserves to be seen, discovered, and experienced.
A city does not have a single story. Every person can add a new experience, story, place, or way of discovering it.

2. General Project Concept

We see the world as a library:

  • Each continent = a shelf
  • Each country = a book (a “volume”) on its continent’s shelf
  • Each city = a chapter in its country’s book
  • Each section = one subject inside the city, filed under one of seven categories (see 5.4)
  • Each contribution = a new story, published as a page of the chapter

For example, the chapter dedicated to Gabès could contain:

  • A traditional dish (Taste)
  • A local song or piece of music (Listen)
  • A tourist or heritage site (Discover)
  • A traditional craft (Create)
  • A local experience or tradition (Experience)
  • A one-day or weekend travel itinerary with a specific budget (Travel)
  • A hidden place that tourists do not usually know about (Local Secret)

The city therefore becomes a collection of different experiences rather than simply a single tourist description.

3. First Phase: The Media Campaign

We start with a media campaign only, without launching the website immediately.

A joint campaign will be launched between JCI ENIG Gabès Sud and JCI Syria, focusing on two cities: Gabès × Latakia.

During the campaign, a series of posts and short videos will be published, allowing the audience to discover the two cities through:

  • Food and local cuisine
  • Music and sounds
  • Places and landmarks
  • Customs and traditions
  • Local experiences
  • Hidden places
  • Budget challenges, such as: “What can you do in this city with a budget of 100 TND?”

Each post is planned so that it can later become a story on the website, under the matching category. The campaign therefore also produces the website’s launch content.

The objective is not to compare the two cities, but to highlight that every city, regardless of how different it is, has stories and experiences worth discovering.

4. Project Reveal

After the content series ends, a final video will reveal that the campaign was only the beginning of a larger project: “A Thousand and One Cities”.

The message will be:

We started with two cities, but the world is full of cities whose stories deserve to be told.

This is when the launch of the digital platform will be announced. The video ends on the website address and shows the library doors opening onto the first two books.

5. Second Phase: Launching the Digital Platform

The website presents the project as a library that visitors walk into. It brings all the campaign stories together, keeps them short and visual, and works the same way on a phone and on a computer.

5.1 Arriving: the library doors

  • The home page opens on a closed double door. It carries the project name in Arabic and English, the line “Every city leaves a story.”, and two archive prints shown as a pair: Gabès (a hand-coloured photograph from 1923) and Latakia (a print of the harbour from 1810).
  • Selecting Enter the library, or tapping the doors, swings both doors open and reveals the reading room behind them.
  • On phones, the title and the Enter button hang on a sign across both doors, so the door opening never cuts through any text or picture.

5.2 The reading room: one shelf per continent

  • Six shelves are always visible: Africa, Asia, Europe, North America, South America and Oceania. Computers show two shelves per row; phones show one shelf per row.
  • At launch, the Africa shelf holds the Tunisia book and the Asia shelf holds the Syria book. Each is marked “More volumes coming soon”.
  • The other shelves read “Coming soon — This shelf is waiting for its first book.” with a Suggest a city button.

5.3 Reading a country book

Selecting a book lifts it off the shelf and opens it. Every book follows the same order of pages:

  1. Cover — the country name in Arabic and English, with a photograph.
  2. Contents — the list of city chapters with their page numbers. It updates automatically whenever a city is added or reordered.
  3. Chapter opening page — one for each city: a photograph, the city name in Arabic and English, and a short introduction.
  4. Section welcome page — one for each section: its category, its title and the list of pages it contains.
  5. Story pages — the stories themselves, in the formats described in 5.4.

Turning pages. Visitors can use the Previous and Next buttons, click the right page to go forward or the left page to go back, swipe on a phone, or use the keyboard arrows. Contents jumps straight to any city, and Back to shelves returns to the library. Computers show the book as a two-page spread; phones show one page at a time with exactly the same content.

5.4 The seven categories and the story formats

Every section of a city chapter is filed under one of seven categories:

CategoryWhat it holdsExample for Gabès
TasteFood, drinks and local productsA traditional dish
ListenMusic and soundsA local song
DiscoverPlaces, monuments and natureThe coastal oasis
CreateCrafts and artA traditional craft
ExperienceTraditions and eventsThe henna souk of Jara
TravelTrips and itineraries by budget and duration“What can you do in Gabès with 100 TND?”
Local SecretWhat tourists usually missA hidden place

Each story has a title, a short description, a city and a category, plus one format:

  • Text — a written story.
  • Image — a photograph with a caption and a credit (author, year and licence).
  • Audio — a song, a sound of the city or a spoken memory, played in the page.
  • Video — a link to YouTube (or an Instagram or Facebook Reel). Videos are never uploaded to the site; the page shows a “Watch on …” link only when a video is supplied.

Itineraries (Travel) are structured pages: a budget, a duration, a timed list of activities with their cost, and a total. For example:

5.6 Finding a city

  • Search — by city or country name, in English or Arabic (for example “Gabès” or “قابس”).
  • Surprise Me — opens a random city chapter. It is always visible in the header, on phones too.
  • The atlas — a world map on the home page with every published city marked. Selecting a city opens its chapter; the rest of the map is left for the cities to come.
  • Our Story — the project concept and the campaign.

5.7 Contributing

  • Contributor account — sign up and sign in with an email address. A contributor can be linked to an organization (for example JCI ENIG Gabès Sud → Gabès, JCI Syria → Latakia), credited on the page as “Chapter created with the contribution of …”.
  • Share a story — choose a city (or propose a new one), a category, a title, a short description and one format.
  • Suggest a city — city, country, why it belongs in the library, optional links, and contact details.
  • Contact — name, email, subject and message.

After sending, the contributor sees “Your story will be reviewed before appearing.” Each contributor can follow the status of their submissions in their account.

5.8 Review and publishing

Nothing appears on the website before an editor has read it. Editors work in a separate admin area with six parts: Overview, Library (books, chapters, sections and pages), Story submissions, City suggestions, Contact inbox and Accounts.

  • Submissions move through pending → approved or rejected → archived; an editor can also ask the contributor for changes.
  • Approving a story or a city suggestion makes it ready to become a draft page or chapter. Publishing is a separate step.
  • Visitors only ever see published content. When a chapter is published, the contents page, search and the atlas update by themselves.
  • Replies to contact messages are prepared as drafts in the admin area.

The content will be short and visual, rather than long articles.

6. Third Phase: Launching the Challenge

After the website is launched, the project will be opened to other JCI organizations.

“Does your city deserve a chapter?”

LOMs will be invited to create a chapter for their city and add content about it. For example:

  • JCI ENIG Gabès Sud → Gabès
  • JCI Syria → Latakia

Other LOMs can then join and add an Italian city, a Jordanian city, a Turkish city, a Moroccan city, and many more.

How an organization joins:

  1. It sends its city through Suggest a city (or the contact form).
  2. The editors review the suggestion and create the city chapter as a draft in its country’s book. If the country has no book yet, a new book is placed on its continent’s shelf.
  3. The organization’s members add stories through Share a story, and the organization is credited on the chapter.
  4. Once published, the chapter appears in the book’s contents, in search and on the atlas.

This is how the project begins to grow, one city at a time.

7. The Role of Social Media After the Website Launch

The media campaign will not stop after the platform is launched. Instead, social media will become a way to highlight the stories available on the website through short-form content such as:

  • “A dish tourists don’t know about”
  • “What can you do with 100 TND?”
  • “If I visited my city for only one day…”

Every post links directly to the matching chapter or story, and each page shows a proper preview (title, description and image) when it is shared on WhatsApp, Facebook, X or Instagram. This creates an integrated connection between the media campaign, the digital platform and the international challenge.

8. What Will Be Achieved in the Current Phase

We will not attempt to implement the project at its final scale from the beginning. Instead, we will present a small pilot version that demonstrates the idea’s potential for expansion. The first phase will include:

  1. Establishing the partnership with JCI Syria
  2. Selecting the Syrian city: Latakia
  3. Defining the content for Gabès and Latakia, category by category
  4. Launching the joint media campaign
  5. Producing a series of short-form content
  6. Revealing “A Thousand and One Cities”
  7. Launching an initial version of the platform: the library described in Section 5
  8. Adding the first two cities
  9. Launching the challenge and inviting other LOMs to participate
  10. Documenting the results and impact

9. Required Resources

The project can be implemented with limited resources:

  • A media team
  • A technical team
  • Available smartphones and computers
  • Social media platforms
  • A simple website, with a domain name and hosting
  • Photographs we own or that are openly licensed, credited on every page
  • One international partner during the first phase

10. Impact and Scalability

The core value of the project does not lie in the number of cities added at the beginning, but in the ability to easily replicate the model.

  • Pilot phase: 2 cities – 2 countries – 2 partnerships
  • Next phase: 10 cities – multiple countries – dozens of contributions
  • Long term: 100 cities, then 1,000 cities and beyond

The ultimate vision is to reach 1,000 cities, thousands of stories, and thousands of ways to discover the world.

11. Core Message

Just as every night in One Thousand and One Nights has a story, every city has its own stories.

“A Thousand and One Cities” is an invitation to discover cities as they are experienced by the people who live there, and not only as they are described in tourist guides.

We begin today with two cities and open the door to a thousand and one cities.
